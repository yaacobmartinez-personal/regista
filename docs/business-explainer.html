Product overview · v1

Every organization runs its own event registration.

Thingstead is a platform where each organization gets its own branded registration space — its own web address, its own events, its own guest lists. People sign up for events in seconds, and organizers manage every attendee from one dashboard. One platform, cleanly separated for every client.

The idea

A place where people gather, organize, and take part.

The name Thingstead comes from Old Norse and Old English: a Thing was an assembly — a gathering where people came together to discuss, decide, and organize — and a stead is a place. Together, a place of assembly. That is the job of the product: an event organizer signs up, claims an address like thingstead.pro/acme, and immediately has a place to publish events and bring their community together.

Behind the scenes it is a single system, but each organization sees only its own world. A university, a conference team, and a community group can all run on Thingstead at the same time and never touch one another's data.

Who it's for

Three people, one flow.

Organizer

Runs the events

Creates events, publishes registration pages, checks attendees in on the day, and invites colleagues to help. Works from a private dashboard.

Registrant

Signs up to attend

A member of the public. Opens an event link, fills a short form, and gets a confirmation email. No account, no friction.

Operator

Runs the platform

That's us. We keep the service running and the data protected — acting on each organization's behalf, never mingling their information.

How it works

From sign-up to check-in, in five steps.

A real sequence — each step unlocks the next.

1

An organization signs up

They create an account, name their organization, and pick their web address. A quick email verification confirms it's really them before the space goes live.

2

They create and publish an event

Title, description, date and time, an optional capacity limit and waitlist. Publishing turns on a public registration page with its own shareable link.

3

The public registers

Attendees fill a short form. Capacity is respected automatically — once full, people join a waitlist or are politely turned away. Everyone gets a confirmation email.

4

Organizers manage attendees

Search the guest list, mark people checked in on the day, and export the list to a spreadsheet. Personal data can be removed on request at any time.

5

They bring in their team

Admins invite colleagues by email and assign a role. The team shares the workload while each person's access stays scoped to that organization.

Built-in safeguards

Trust is part of the product, not an add-on.

These protections are designed in from day one — the reassurances a client will ask about before they put their guests' details in.

✓

Strict separation

No organization can ever see or touch another's events, attendees, or team.

✓

Verified sign-ups

New organizations confirm their email, and public endpoints are rate-limited against abuse.

✓

Instant access control

Remove a teammate and their access ends immediately — roles are checked on every action.

✓

Right to be forgotten

A registrant's personal details can be truly erased while attendance counts stay accurate.

✓

Clear accountability

Exports and deletions of personal data are recorded, so there's always a trail.

✓

Privacy by design

A privacy notice at sign-up, encrypted connections, and a GDPR-aware data model.
